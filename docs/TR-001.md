## Abstract

This report documents the initial hardware characterization phase of a TinyML-oriented embedded systems project based on an ESP32 microcontroller and a commercially available IMU module sold as an MPU-6050.

The objective was to establish reliable I2C communication between the ESP32 and the IMU, validate low-level register access, identify the actual device through direct register interrogation, and obtain preliminary accelerometer measurements suitable for future dataset generation.

Unexpectedly, the sensor did not identify itself as an MPU-6050 — the WHO_AM_I register returned `0x72` instead of the expected `0x68`. Despite this, successful communication, register manipulation, and accelerometer data acquisition were achieved without the use of vendor libraries.

---

## 1. Introduction

TinyML applications rely heavily on the quality and reliability of sensor data. Before constructing datasets or training machine learning models, it is necessary to validate the complete sensing pipeline:

- Physical hardware
- Electrical connections
- Communication interfaces
- Sensor initialization
- Data acquisition

This report focuses exclusively on the hardware validation and low-level communication stage.

## 4. Experimental Procedure

### 4.1 I2C Bus Validation

An I2C address scan was performed to verify device presence on the bus.

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Detected address | `0x68` |

The sensor successfully responded to I2C discovery requests.

### 4.2 Device Identification

The WHO_AM_I register was queried directly.

| Register | Value |
|---|---|
| Address | `0x75` |
| Observed | `0x72` |
| Expected (MPU-6050) | `0x68` |

The device does not identify itself as a genuine MPU-6050. It is likely a pin-compatible replacement or a newer member of the InvenSense/TDK inertial sensor family.

### 4.3 Power Management Inspection

| Register | Initial value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `0x6B` | `0x40` | Sensor in sleep/low-power state |

### 4.4 Sensor Activation

Value `0x00` was written to register `0x6B`. A subsequent read confirmed the write, verifying bidirectional register communication and successful transition to active state.

### 4.5 Accelerometer Data Acquisition

Raw acceleration registers `0x3B`–`0x40` were read directly to obtain AX, AY, AZ.

**Representative measurements:**

| Axis | Value |
|---|---|
| AX | −1876 |
| AY | 524 |
| AZ | 17556 |

Additional samples confirmed stable operation and repeatability.

---

## 5. Preliminary Observations

Stationary noise observed:

| Axis | Approximate range |
|---|---|
| AX | −1900 ± 100 |
| AY | 500 ± 70 |
| AZ | 17600 ± 150 |

Fluctuations are consistent with expected MEMS sensor behavior. No evidence of communication instability was observed.

---

## 6. Key Findings

The following were successfully demonstrated:

- I2C communication
- Register reading and writing
- Sensor wake-up sequence
- Raw accelerometer acquisition
- Stable serial data transmission

The installed device does not match the identity of a standard MPU-6050 despite sharing its I2C address and register layout.
